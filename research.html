Research

Learning to assist with AI and robots in the real world.

Background in embodied AI, AI-based decision making, language understanding, assistive technologies, multimodal AI and human-centred AI. Applications in healthcare, manufacturing and agriculture.

Communicating with humans

Bridging communication gaps with common-sense, personalisation and training support.

  • Multimodal content generation for people with language impairment (aphasia), with a user study with speech-and-language therapists.
  • Prediction of child–caregiver interaction with multimodal AI.
  • Virtual role simulator (ongoing): a research platform for studying and training empathy in meaningful conversations, combining a large language model, an empathy-assessment framework (EPITOME), speech and 3D avatars, in workplace, education and healthcare scenarios.
  • Empathic Home (ongoing): AI models of a home or building that aim to improve the well-being of its occupants.
  • Adaptive social interaction with the MiRo-E robot; human–drone interfaces for post-disaster response; outdoor robot guides for visually impaired people.
  • Earlier work at NICT: generating natural-language descriptions and instructions for domestic service robots.

Perceiving and understanding the world

Solving the ambiguities of the world with multimodal perception.

IROS 2026

PhyPush: one push is all you need for sensorless physical property estimation with physics-guided transformers. Estimating the physical properties of an object from a single push, without force sensing. arXiv

  • Multimodal language understanding for domestic service robots (NICT, 2017–2021). Disambiguating instructions such as "Put away the milk and cereal" from language, vision and context (MMC-GAN); reducing the labelling burden with latent-space data augmentation and automatically generated instructions; explaining predictions with attention, for example the risk of collision when placing an object (PonNet); vision-and-language navigation (CrossMap Transformer).
  • Robot audition and aural servo (PhD, Inria/IRISA, 2013–2016). Controlling the robot's motion directly from auditory cues such as interaural level and time differences, instead of localising the sound source first. Because the control relies on the variation of the cues rather than on their absolute values, it needs no model of the room acoustics or of the robot's head.

Physically interacting with the world

Interacting safely with complex objects in complex environments.

  • Deep reinforcement learning for mobile robots: whole-body control for mobile manipulation in complex environments, and precise, safe manoeuvring of double-Ackermann-steering robots (ManeuverNet, ICRA 2026).
  • Deformable linear object manipulation. Controlling large 3D deformations of cables, hoses and plant stems with deep reinforcement learning, for example MultiAC6, a multi actor-critic framework that decomposes the action space. This line of work started in the EU H2020 project ACROBA.
  • Physics-structured policies. Screw Attention, a transformer layer in which the relation between two bodies is a spatial transform, so that the policy does not depend on how reference frames are attached to the robot's links.
  • Assistive caregiving. Robot-assisted bed bathing with the RoboNotts team, winner of the ICRA 2025 Physical Robotic Caregiving Challenge.
